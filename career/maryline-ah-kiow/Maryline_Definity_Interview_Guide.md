## Claims language

Maryline does not need to sound like an adjuster. She needs plain, accurate words, and a clear limit on what this role decides.

**What is a first notice of loss?**

A first notice of loss, or FNOL, is the first report that an incident happened. In this role I would collect the facts, open the claim in the system, give the customer the contact details, and explain the next step. I would not settle the claim on that call.

**What is a claim?**

A claim is the customer’s request for the insurer to respond after a loss. My job at the first step is to record it completely and accurately.

**What is coverage?**

Coverage is what the policy responds to. I would give only the basic information Definity has approved for this role. If I was not sure the loss was covered, I would say that I need to confirm it. I would not promise a payment.

**A customer asks, “Will you pay for this?”**

I understand why you want to know. I can explain the next step and the basic information I am allowed to give. I cannot promise that the claim will be paid until it has been reviewed under the policy. I will make sure the file has the facts and that you know who follows up.

**What if you do not know the answer?**

I would not guess, especially about coverage or money. I would tell the customer that I want the correct information, I would check the approved resource or the right team, and I would come back with a clear update.

**What does InsurCloud refer to?**

It is the claims system named in the posting, where the associate enters the claim and sets up the file. Maryline has not used it. The honest line is: I have not used InsurCloud yet. I have learned other systems, including SAP, and I would learn this one in training before I relied on it with a customer.

## Role-play lines

### A customer is upset that help is taking too long

- “I understand how frustrating it is to wait, especially when you need to know what happens next.”
- Confirm who they are and look up the file in the approved system.
- Say what is already done, what is still missing, and what the next step is.
- Give a realistic time for the next update. Document the call and keep ownership of any follow-up you promised.

### A customer wants an answer you do not have

- “I want to make sure I give you the correct information, so I am going to verify that.”
- Use the approved resource or the right internal team.
- Return with a clear answer or a realistic next step.
- Do not guess, and do not hide the uncertainty.

### A customer does not understand a term, such as deductible or first notice of loss

- Acknowledge the question without making them feel foolish.
- Give a short explanation and one simple example from the approved material.
- Check: “Would it help if I explained how that applies to this claim?”
- Stay inside the role. Do not give a personal opinion about whether they should have bought different coverage.

### The customer may need a supplemental service

- Confirm what the process allows before offering it.
- Do not promise a tow, a rental, or another service unless the procedure says this role arranges it.
- Explain what you can arrange now and what another team must approve.
- Document what was requested and what was arranged.

### Two urgent calls at once

- Look at immediate safety, deadlines, and the risk of a wrong or incomplete file.
- Take the higher-risk call first.
- Acknowledge the second call and set an expectation so it is not lost.
- Document both, and ask for help if the team needs to cover one of them.

- What does strong performance look like in the first three months?
- What does a typical first-notice call include, and when does this role hand the file to the next team?
- How is training organized for someone new to insurance, including InsurCloud and systems such as Guidewire, before they take calls alone?
- How does the team support an associate when a caller is distressed or angry?
- The posting sets shifts between 8:00 a.m. and 4:30 p.m. How are those hours assigned?
- The posting describes hybrid work for most roles. How does this team divide office time and virtual time, and which Ontario office is it based in?
- What are the next steps in the process?

Ask two or three, not the whole list. The training question and the handoff question are the strongest.

## What the Wealthsimple guide contains that this guide deliberately leaves out

Copying these into a Definity interview would pull the conversation toward the wrong job.

- Investing explanations: TFSA and RRSP, stocks, ETFs, diversification, risk tolerance, and “What should I invest in?” Definity needs FNOL, coverage boundaries, and “I will not promise payment” instead.
- The 100 percent remote pitch: private workspace, home internet, MacBook and equipment, and the work-from-anywhere allowance. This Definity posting says hybrid for most roles, with time in the office and time online depending on the work. It does not promise a fully remote job. Wealthsimple’s evening-and-weekend schedule also does not match. This posting’s window is 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. Eastern.
- Slack, many browser tabs, and keyboard shortcuts as proof of technical fit. For Definity, the relevant systems point is SAP and databases now, then InsurCloud and Guidewire in Definity’s training. Do not claim she already uses CDS or Guidewire.
- Fintech curiosity as the reason for the move. The Definity reason is service at a stressful moment, plus careful records.
- Role-plays about a slow institutional transfer or a promotion deadline. Replace them with a delayed claim update, an unknown coverage answer, a supplemental service, and two urgent calls.
- Wealthsimple pay ($25.00 to $26.50 an hour) and benefits (health spending account, employer savings match, vacation, sick days). Use Definity’s range of $49,300 to $82,100. The LinkedIn posting lists share ownership, a discretionary incentive, pension and matched RRSP contributions, volunteer days, tuition assistance, and wellness programs. It gives no dollar amounts for those items, so she should not invent any, and she should not open the interview with benefits.
- “Why Wealthsimple makes finance easier for Canadians” as the motivation line.
